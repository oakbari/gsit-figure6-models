# Figure 6 legend

**Figure 6. Generic effects of male mating competitiveness, fertility and
population growth on reproductive output and release requirements.** All
parameter values are illustrative and were not fitted to the experimental
strains. Relative viable-offspring output is `G=(1+crf)/(1+cr)`, where `c` is
released-male mating competitiveness relative to wild type, `r` is the ratio of
released to wild males available to mate, and `f` is relative fertility following
a released-male mating.

**(A)** Reproductive-output reduction, `100(1-G)`, across competitiveness
`c=0–1` and male ratio `r=0–50`, with complete sterility (`f=0`). White contours
mark 90% and 95% reproductive reduction.

**(B)** Reproductive-output reduction across `c=0–1` and relative fertility
`f=0–20%`, at an illustrative ratio `r=10`. White contours mark 50% and 75%
reproductive reduction. Panels A and B use the same linear percentage scale.

**(C)** Maintained male ratio required for at least 99% equilibrium adult-female
suppression across `c=0–1` and `f=0–1%`, assuming an illustrative low-density
adult-daughter multiplier `R0=4`. The population model is
`F_next=R0*G*F/[1+(R0-1)*G*F/K]`, where `K` is untreated equilibrium female
abundance. For a target equilibrium fraction `a=0.01`, define
`H=R0-a*(R0-1)`; the required ratio is `(H-1)/[c*(1-H*f)]`. A finite ratio exists
only for `c>0` and `f<1/H`. The displayed fertility range ends at 1%, below the feasibility boundary
`f=1/3.97`, approximately 25.19%. At or above that boundary, no finite male ratio
achieves the target. Curved dashed contours mark maintained released-to-wild
male ratios of 10:1 and 50:1.

**(D)** The maintained ratio for the same 99% adult-female suppression target
across `c=0–1` and growth multipliers `R0=2–10`, assuming complete sterility.
Here the required ratio simplifies to `0.99*(R0-1)/c`. Dashed contours mark maintained released-to-wild male ratios
of 5:1, 10:1 and 20:1. Panels C and D share a logarithmic ratio scale; values at or
below 1 and at or above 1,000 use the respective end colors. Numeric grid values
are uncapped. A positive target cannot be achieved at `c=0`.

The mating model assumes random effective mating, one effective mating per
female and equal fecundity across mate classes. Population panels assume a
constant effective male ratio, no immigration and saturating density regulation
acting on viable recruitment. They omit spatial and age structure, seasonality,
dormant eggs, stochasticity and explicit construct inheritance. Fertility is not
a parental sorting-error rate. Panels A–B describe one mating cohort, whereas
C–D describe deterministic population equilibria; none estimates extinction
probability, field efficacy, release frequency or factory throughput.

In panel C, f is interpreted as the fraction of fully fertile males in the final release cohort; all other released males are completely sterile. Fertile contaminants are assumed to have wild-type fertility and the same mating competitiveness as sterile released males. Under these assumptions, f equals mean relative fertility following a released-male mating. It is distinct from the parental sorting-error rate.
